# Methodology — DeepThought Target Company Research

## Scope and assumption
I treated the Vadodara–Ahmedabad belt as one practical specialty-chemicals sourcing corridor because many companies in this segment have registered offices, plants, R&D teams, and customers distributed across the same Gujarat industrial ecosystem. The strict Part A city requirement should be finalized by keeping only the Vadodara rows or only the Ahmedabad rows if the reviewer insists on a single municipal city.

Primary segments:
- Custom synthesis & specialty chemicals
- Performance chemicals

## Research process
1. Built an initial universe from search queries around “Vadodara specialty chemicals manufacturer”, “Ahmedabad performance chemicals manufacturer”, company directories, MCA-style company pages, LinkedIn company pages, and company websites.
2. Removed obvious non-fits: traders, distributors, no website, service-only players, large group subsidiaries, recently acquired companies, and companies apparently above the ₹500Cr revenue ceiling.
3. Scored each company against the six Federer criteria:
   - C1 Manufacturer
   - C2 India-based
   - C3 Differentiated
   - C4 Technical decision-maker
   - C5 Growing sector
   - C6 Active growth signals
4. Used Strong / Moderate / Weak scoring, then converted to the weighted Federer Score.
5. Wrote one specific personalization hook per company to test whether the research is outreach-ready.

## Important quality caveat
This is an open-source first pass. For a final recruiter submission, I would manually verify:
- latest revenue from MCA/Tofler/paid database/annual report,
- ownership/control and PE/acquisition status,
- plant location vs sales office,
- decision-maker technical background from LinkedIn, company pages, interviews, and MCA filings,
- recent growth signals such as hiring, new certification, new plant, export expansion, or current website/news activity.

## What I learned about the segment
Vadodara and Ahmedabad have many specialty chemical manufacturers, but evidence depth varies a lot. Listed/public companies are easy to validate but often exceed the ₹500Cr ceiling. Smaller private companies may fit the Federer mindset better, but they need manual verification for revenue and technical decision-maker background.
